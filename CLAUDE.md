# Code conventions

- A virtual method returning Task whose base implementation does nothing is declared async with an empty body, as ServeInventoryToken in GetDataMessage: `protected virtual async Task ServeInventoryToken(Peer peer, Inventory inventory) { }`, never with `return Task.CompletedTask;`.
- In forms and panels, a field's label generally stands to the right of its field, so that the fields stay vertically aligned with each other.
- A form closes with an X button in its top right corner, never with a "Close" button.

# Rendering pitfalls

- An ItemsControl or a ContentControl clips whatever its content draws beyond its bounds, of any shape and kind, such as a shadow, a glow, a blur or a decoration that sticks out, because ClipToBounds is True on the control itself, not on its template's Border or presenter, even when the control has a ControlTheme of its own. Before letting content draw outside its control's bounds, switch the clipping off by setting ClipToBounds to False on that control, as ThemeBlockchain in MiningView.axaml does for the blocks' ItemsControl, instead of extending the control with margins. A standalone ContentPresenter is no replacement for a ContentControl, because it makes its own DataContext the content, which breaks a binding of its Content to the surrounding DataContext.
- Every three-dimensional shape of the GUI, round ones included, follows one law: an orthographic view, a camera with parallel sight lines perpendicular to the screen, placed 26.57 degrees to the right of the front (tan = 1/2) and 24.09 degrees above it (sin = 1/√6), looking back at the scene without roll. A point with x to the right, y down and z into depth lands on the screen at X = 0.8944 x + 0.4472 z and Y = 0.1826 x + 0.9129 y − 0.3651 z. A box's edges along x thus run 0.8944 to the right and 0.1826 down per unit, vertical edges stay vertical at 0.9129 of their length, and edges into depth run 0.4472 to the right and 0.3651 up per unit.
- In this view a sphere of radius r is a circle of radius r. A horizontal circle of radius r around cx,cy is an unrotated ellipse with semi-axes r horizontally and 0.4082 r vertically; its front half runs from the left point to the right point with sweep 0: `M cx−r,cy A r,0.4082r 0 0 0 cx+r,cy`. A vertical cylinder's sides are vertical lines at cx ± r. A vertical cone whose apex lies a below its base circle's center on the screen, a being 0.9129 times its true height, or a frustum whose virtual apex does, has its sides touch the base ellipse at cx ± r·√(1 − (0.4082 r / a)²), (0.4082 r)²/a below the center, provided a exceeds 0.4082 r; for an apex above the center, the touch points lie above it. In general, a circle whose plane has the normal n becomes an ellipse with semi-axes r and r·|n·d|, d being the view direction (−1, 1, 2)/√6, with the short axis along n's image: on a front face semi-axes r and 0.8165 r with the long axis rotated 50.77 degrees, on a right face r and 0.4082 r rotated −78.46 degrees. Flat content on a face maps with a MatrixTransform and RenderTransformOrigin 0,0: on a front face 0.8944, 0.1826, 0, 0.9129, 0, 0, on a top face 0.8944, 0.1826, 0.4472, −0.3651, 0, 0, and on a right face 0.4472, −0.3651, 0, 0.9129, 0, 0.
- The light comes from the upper right, from the direction (1, −2, −1)/√6, to the right, up and toward the viewer. Faces drawn with opacities of an accent color get 0.68 on top faces, 0.5 on right faces and 0.3 on front faces; inner faces seen through glass follow the same rule by the direction their surface faces, so an inner left wall counts as a right face and an inner floor as a top face. Curved glass and metal brighten toward their right side, as BrushGlass in MiningView.axaml does, and a metal gradient across a shape puts its bright stop at 0.7. White highlight strokes sit on a shape's right side. Every drop shadow falls to the lower left: it is the shape's silhouette filled black at opacity 0.35 with a BlurEffect of radius 8, shifted 10 to the left and 10 down by Margin -10,10,10,-10.
- Where shapes overlap, the nearer one is drawn on top, the one with the larger x − y − 2z. For shapes at the same height and depth, the one further right lies in front; on the rail, a newer block is drawn on top of the older one.
